Mainnet: what to expect

This sandbox teaches you the model of a Turkish TRY ⇄ USDC ramp. A production anchor keeps the concepts and changes the mechanics — in predictable ways. This page lists what carries over unchanged and what a production anchor makes real behind the same SEP surface, so you know what to expect before you ship.

Everything here is generalized from how production Turkish anchors and exchanges run a TRY ⇄ USDC ramp. Every anchor differs in detail — when yours publishes its stellar.toml and docs, those win.

SEP → production: mostly a config change. You built on the SEP path, so going live changes only two things: the network passphrase (public) and the home domain (the real anchor's). The SEP-1/10/6/12/38 endpoint shapes are the standard and do not change, so your transport and wire calls stay the same. The one dependency: the mainnet anchor must implement SEP-6. What you do build on your side: the real KYC step — collecting and submitting SEP-12 fields, handling the NEEDS_INFO loop — and rendering the extra statuses/rejections a production anchor returns. Everything below is what the anchor makes real behind that same standard surface.

What carries over unchanged

  • The deposit-reference model. TRY still arrives by bank transfer to the anchor's IBAN with a reference code in the description (açıklama). Matched → credited; unmatched → a support path. Your UI for "show IBAN + reference" survives as is.
  • Balance-and-ledger thinking. Fiat in → balance → convert → on-chain out (and the reverse). Reconciliation reads a ledger/transaction list.
  • Quote → execute. A priced offer with an expiry, then a commitment against it.
  • Address + memo routing for incoming crypto on Stellar, and IBAN payouts for fiat out.
  • Status machines. Every transaction walks named states; your job is to render and react to all of them, including the ugly ones (unmatched, refunded, failed).

What will likely change

The endpoint shapes don't change; the anchor makes what was simulated real. On your side that mainly means implementing the real SEP-12 KYC step and handling the extra statuses below — not re-plumbing the ramp.

1 · KYC and compliance become real (SEP-12)

Here SEP-12 auto-approves and stores no personal data. A production anchor verifies a real identity through the same SEP-12 endpoint, and adds compliance fields the standard already carries:

  • Real identity data collected and verified before ramping; KYC tiers that set your limits.
  • Travel rule: originator information on crypto deposits (name, address ownership, VASP fields).
  • Purpose & source of funds on withdrawals; 2FA on sensitive steps; compliance rejections as first-class states.
  • Pre-registration: withdrawal addresses and bank accounts may need to be saved and verified before use.

2 · Real bank rails replace the simulate step

The simulate-bank-transfer helper disappears. A real TRY bank transfer to the anchor's IBAN, with the reference in the açıklama, credits the deposit — you observe it move the SEP-6 transaction to completed, you do not trigger it. The unmatched-transfer support path still matters, so keep that UI.

3 · Live pricing (SEP-38)

  • Real quotes: SEP-38 prices come from a real order book, expire faster, and move with liquidity; commissions may be tiered by volume.
  • Fees as line items: network withdrawal fee, spread / commission, sometimes a fixed payout fee — not one blended spread.

4 · Limits and operations

  • Limits: per-transaction and per-tier minimums/maximums on every leg.
  • Rate limits (HTTP 429) with backoff; idempotency discipline on writes.
  • Real incident behaviour: maintenance windows (503), partial outages, delayed settlement. Your state machine, not your happy path, is the product.

5 · Money details

  • Mainnet USDC issuer is GA5ZSEJYB37JRC5AVCIA5MOP4RHTM335X2KGX3IHOJAPP5RE34K4KZVN — trustlines, treasury addresses and explorers all change; never hardcode the testnet issuer.
  • All of this is discovered from the mainnet anchor's stellar.toml, exactly as on testnet.
